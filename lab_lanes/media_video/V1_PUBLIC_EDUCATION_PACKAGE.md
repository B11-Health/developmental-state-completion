# V1 Scientific Explainer Package — Developmental State Completion

## Evidence-safe framing
Core question: when two developing organisms look similar now, does a richer measurement of their present state make older measured history stop adding stable predictive value for a specified future/intervention family? If not, can targeted perturbations resolve the remaining mechanism ambiguity better than baseline-only or equal-cost/random alternatives?

Current support is observational and computational. Living-plant perturbational validation remains the decisive next step. The historical 224-world generator is provenance-incomplete and was not recovered. The later 128-world source-validation bundle is reproducible. Do not claim universal Markovity, one-dimensional biology, a new definition of state, new topology, mathematical priority, or completed living-plant validation.

## 4–6 minute YouTube explainer
**Working title:** Can a Plant’s Present State Replace Its History?

1. 0:00–0:35 — The puzzle: similar-looking present states can hide different predictive information. Avoid saying shape “lies”; say morphology can be an incomplete measurement.
2. 0:35–1:20 — Define developmental-state completion operationally: for a specified prediction/intervention task, test whether adding older measured history to a present-state measurement yields stable out-of-sample predictive gain.
3. 1:20–2:05 — Observed-data result: direct-source Arabidopsis FM1 reanalysis shows a strong late-L1 screening pattern; an earlier window is estimator-dependent. Mention grouped splits/leakage controls. Do not generalize beyond the tested task and dataset.
4. 2:05–2:50 — Incompleteness control: lineage data where history remains useful demonstrates the diagnostic can fail, which is necessary for falsifiability.
5. 2:50–3:35 — Residual ambiguity: if current measurements do not screen off history, represent remaining state–law alternatives at finite resolution and ask which feasible perturbation separates them most efficiently. Emphasize precedents in observability, predictive-state methods, active design, and topology.
6. 3:35–4:20 — Computational validation: the later 128-world source-validation bundle is reproducible and prospectively recovers hidden signed state under designed contexts. Historical 224-world topology/simulator claims are downgraded because the original generator/source bundle is provenance-incomplete.
7. 4:20–5:10 — What this is NOT: not proof that biology is one-dimensional; not universal Markovity; not a new definition of state; not mathematical priority; not living-plant validation.
8. 5:10–5:50 — Decisive experiment: blinded plant work comparing designed perturbations with baseline-only and equal-cost/random alternatives, with frozen analysis and falsification criteria.
