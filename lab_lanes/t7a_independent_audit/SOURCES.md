# T7A Sources and Prior-Art Boundary

Research was performed only in the isolated CDP 9444 browser.

## Authoritative / primary mathematical anchors

- Karol Borsuk (1933), *Drei Saetze ueber die n-dimensionale euklidische Sphaere*, Fundamenta Mathematicae 20, 177-190. Classical antipodal coincidence theorem used by T7.1.
- C.-T. Yang / Bourgin-Yang literature: classical extensions controlling coincidence sets; relevant context for quantitative forms, but not required for T7's exact risk-1 proof.
- E. Fadell and S. Husseini (1988), ideal-valued cohomological index theory, a standard equivariant obstruction framework.
- M. Gromov (2003), *Isoperimetry of waists and concentration of maps*, GAFA 13, 178-215; later sphere-waist work such as Memarian. Stronger quantitative fiber-size context.
- S. A. Melikhov and related projected/near-projected embedding work: `k`-prem terminology for lifting a map by Euclidean coordinates to an embedding. This overlaps the exact full-fiber-separation problem `(p,B)`.
- Standard covering-space texts such as Hatcher for local triviality and the usual Hausdorff/CW setting. The T7 compact-margin proof should explicitly retain a Hausdorff-type hypothesis rather than rely on convention.
- Standard optimal-transport theory for `W_p`: metric structure on probability measures with finite `p`th moment over standard metric spaces; mixture-coupling inequalities are elementary consequences of couplings.

## Additional overlap found during adversarial search

Search for “Urysohn width continuous map fiber diameter minimax approximation metric space” recovered the established Urysohn-width literature, which measures how well compact metric spaces can be represented by low-dimensional complexes via control of fibers. This is not identical to T7's task-relative `(h,B,F)` risk, but it makes broad novelty claims about a newly invented quantitative topological representation dimension unsafe without a dedicated comparison.

Search for quantitative/stability versions of Borsuk-Ulam recovered modern work relating Borsuk-Ulam constraints to Gromov-Hausdorff distance and Vietoris-Rips complexes, as well as the older Dubins-Schwarz quantitative-discontinuity tradition. T7's sphere risk theorem is simpler: it uses one exact Borsuk-Ulam collision plus the triangle inequality and should be presented as that corollary, not as a new quantitative Borsuk-Ulam theorem.

## Safe classification

- **Known theorem:** Borsuk-Ulam; covering local triviality; Wasserstein/TV metric facts; compactness/extreme-value principle.
- **Straightforward corollary:** exact risk 1 from a forced antipodal collision; pairwise `delta/2` collision risk; bounded-noise nearest-neighbor threshold for a finite codebook.
- **Project-specific formulation/synthesis:** `R_k`, epsilon-completion dimension relative to a declared present map and future target; robust predictive margin attached to dangerous same-present pairs; integration with developmental-state completion.
- **Questionable if advertised as novel topology:** quantitative topological dimension broadly construed, projected/fiberwise embedding criteria, generic robust-recovery thresholds, or Urysohn-width-like low-dimensional representation ideas.
