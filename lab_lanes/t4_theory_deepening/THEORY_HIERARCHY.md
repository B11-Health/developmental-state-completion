# Theory hierarchy for developmental state sufficiency

Status: adversarial theory note. No priority claim.

## Setup
Let H denote measured pre-present history, X the latent present system, S=h(X) the measured present representation, A a future intervention or policy from a declared family Pi, and Y^{a}_{0:T} the future observable outcome under intervention a. Statements below are always relative to an observation sigma-field, intervention family, horizon, and loss/metric.

## H0. Loss-relative predictive sufficiency
For loss L and decision class D, S is L-sufficient for H with respect to Y under a if the Bayes risk using S equals that using (S,H). This is weaker than conditional independence except for strictly proper losses rich enough to identify the full predictive law.

## H1. Distributional predictive sufficiency
S is distributionally predictive-sufficient for a if P(Y^a in . | H,S)=P(Y^a in . | S) almost surely. Equivalently H -> S -> Y^a is a Markov chain under the interventional law. Under log loss this is equivalent to I(Y^a;H|S)=0 when regular conditional laws exist.

## H2. Intervention-indexed predictive sufficiency
S is Pi-sufficient when H1 holds simultaneously for every a in Pi. A single observational regime is only one member of Pi and does not imply this property for untested interventions.

## H3. Finite-horizon sufficiency
S is (Pi,T)-sufficient when H2 holds for the trajectory Y^a_{0:T}. If the family of finite-dimensional laws is projectively consistent, sufficiency for every finite T implies equality of the full path-space law. Sufficiency at one finite T does not imply longer-horizon or asymptotic sufficiency.

## H4. Approximate/tolerance sufficiency
Choose divergence d on conditional future laws. S is epsilon-(Pi,T)-sufficient if ess sup_{a in Pi} d(P(Y^a_{0:T}|H,S),P(Y^a_{0:T}|S)) <= epsilon. Prediction-error tolerances are decoder/loss dependent; Wasserstein, total variation, KL and excess Bayes risk are not interchangeable without assumptions.

## H5. Predictive equivalence / causal-state quotient
Histories h1,h2 are equivalent when they induce identical future distributions for the declared tests/interventions. The quotient is the coarsest predictive state for that test family. This is the computational-mechanics / predictive-state idea and is not claimed as new here.

## H6. Mechanistic / causal completeness
A representation is mechanistically complete only relative to a structural model class: it must determine the variables needed to propagate all admissible interventions and outcomes, not merely screen off H for one task. Mechanistic completeness generally implies intervention-indexed predictive sufficiency for outcomes generated by the model, but the converse fails because distinct mechanisms can be interventionally equivalent on Pi.

## Implications that do hold
1. Mechanistic completeness for a correct structural model and intervention family => Pi-predictive sufficiency for its generated observables.
2. Pi-predictive sufficiency => predictive sufficiency for every subfamily Pi' subset Pi.
3. Path-law sufficiency => finite-horizon sufficiency for every T.
4. Exact distributional sufficiency => zero Bayes value of H for every proper scoring rule whose Bayes act depends only on the predictive law.
5. If a finite experiment family separates every pair of candidate worlds in a finite hypothesis class, the joint response signature identifies the world/equivalence class exactly in the noiseless model.

## Non-implications
1. Observational screening-off !=> interventional screening-off.
2. One-loss sufficiency !=> distributional sufficiency.
3. Finite-horizon sufficiency !=> asymptotic/path sufficiency.
4. Predictive sufficiency !=> biological Markovity of the full organism.
5. Low-dimensional sufficient S !=> low-dimensional latent mechanism.
6. Local differential kernel inclusion !=> global injectivity or global factorization without topological/regularity assumptions.
7. Dense empirical test coverage !=> exact causal-state identification under finite noise/sample size.

## Differential geometry interface
Let F:X->R^m be the stacked future-response map for the declared intervention panel and h:X->R^k the present measurement. A necessary infinitesimal condition for F to factor locally through h is ker Dh_x subset ker DF_x. If h is a submersion and F is constant on connected fibers of h in a neighborhood, then F locally factors as F=g o h. Kernel inclusion alone only says DF annihilates tangent directions invisible to h; disconnected fibers or nonintegrable/global topology can still violate global factorization.

A useful sufficient global condition is: h:X->S is a surjective submersion, every fiber h^{-1}(s) is connected, and ker Dh subset ker DF everywhere. Then F is constant on each fiber (integrate derivative along fiber paths) and there exists a unique set map g with F=g o h; standard quotient/submersion regularity gives smooth g under usual manifold hypotheses. This turns the local kernel condition into task-level deterministic sufficiency for F, not stochastic/mechanistic completeness.

## Approximate fiber stability
If observed response maps F and Fhat satisfy ||F-Fhat||_inf <= eta, then pairwise sup distances change by at most 2 eta. Hence the true delta-survivor relation is sandwiched between estimated thresholds delta-2eta and delta+2eta. Connected components are stable only when no critical inter-world edge distance lies inside this 2eta uncertainty band. Without a margin, arbitrarily small perturbations can merge or split threshold graphs.

## Empirical reading for this repository
The defensible theory is task-, intervention-family-, horizon-, loss-, decoder-, measurement- and calibration-relative. R1/R3/M2/M3 support diagnostic and design machinery; they do not establish universal state completion. A newly tested intervention that restores calibrated history value is evidence that the current measured state is incomplete for the enlarged family, not evidence that predictive-state ideas themselves fail.
