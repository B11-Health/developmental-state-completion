## Individualized draft notes — DO NOT SEND

### Tsiantis group
Subject: Feasibility questions for a small prospective RCOg-V / ChCUC1-PIN1 pilot

Professor Tsiantis, we are preparing a deliberately small prospective test built around the Wang RCOg-V suppressor panel and the Hu dex-inducible ChCUC1/PIN1 response assay. Before preregistering confirmatory acquisition, we want to determine whether the exact materials can be combined without distorting the biology. Could your group advise on current availability/transfer terms for RCOg-V, cuc2-4;RCOg-V and either cyp71-3;RCOg-V or nop2a-5;RCOg-V, plus the representative Hu inducible/PIN1:GFP line identities and marker information? We would also value your judgment on GFP/VENUS compatibility and the smallest meaningful dex-burden control. We are not asking you to endorse the framework; a feasibility “no” is equally useful.

### Laufs group
Subject: Independent feasibility review — CUC1/PIN1 leaf perturbation pilot

Dr Laufs, your work and the Hu study make your group especially relevant to a planned Arabidopsis leaf-morphogenesis feasibility test. We are considering transferring the published 24-h dex-inducible ChCUC1/PIN1 polarity assay into RCOg-V suppressor backgrounds. Before doing so, could you flag the main biological failure modes you would expect—stage matching, reporter interpretation, induction burden, or CUC-dependent morphology—and whether you know the provenance/transfer route for the representative Hu inducible lines? No data or material availability is being assumed.

### Runions group
Subject: Blinded quantitative leaf-response pipeline — feasibility advice

Professor Runions, we are preregistering a small living-system test that would compare complete baseline imaging with a 24-h perturbation-response vector in Arabidopsis leaf 4. Because your group works on computational morphodynamics and collaborates with the Tsiantis programme, we would value advice on a minimal frozen geometry/cell-polarity feature set, developmental-stage matching, and QC rules that can be implemented before genotype unblinding. The goal is to prevent feature engineering after labels are known, not to maximize separation.

### Bhatia route
Subject: Practical RCOg-V / CUC-PIN1 assay compatibility questions

Dr Bhatia, because you contributed to both the RCOg-V suppressor work and the CUC1/PIN1 study, we would value a practical reality check on combining those systems. In particular: were RCOg-V and the Hu inducible/PIN1 reporters ever present in the same background, which suppressor is easiest to propagate, and what imaging or staging failures would you consider disqualifying? We are treating all current material availability as unknown and would coordinate any formal request through the appropriate originating lab.

### Hu route
Subject: Reproducing the 24-h ChCUC1/PIN1 assay in a new background

Dr Hu, we are evaluating whether the published Arabidopsis 10-uM dex ChCUC1/PIN1 assay can be transferred into a small set of RCOg-V suppressor backgrounds. Could you identify the two representative inducible lines used in the paper and any handling details not explicit in the SI (spray amount, staging window, timing, acquisition/QC) that are essential for reproducibility? We are especially interested in a treatment-engagement criterion that does not depend on genotype separation.

### Scarpella lab
Subject: Independent PIN1 reporter-interpretation check

Professor Scarpella, we are designing a blinded Arabidopsis leaf experiment in which a functional PIN1:GFP polarity response is an acute readout. We have excluded a pin1 suppressor class because introducing functional PIN1:GFP could alter the causal background itself. We would value a brief independent assessment of that exclusion and of the safest polarity features/QC controls for comparing developing leaves across genetic backgrounds. No request for proprietary material is implied.
