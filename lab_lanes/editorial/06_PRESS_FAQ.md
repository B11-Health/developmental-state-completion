# Press-style FAQ

## What is the project trying to test?

The project asks a task-specific question: for a defined developmental prediction/intervention problem, is a physically measurable present state sufficient to predict the specified future, or does older measured history still add reproducible predictive information? If history still matters, the present representation is treated as incomplete and the next experiment is chosen to expose the remaining ambiguity.

## Is this a new definition of biological state?

No. Predictive-state representations, causal states, input-output computational mechanics, observability, and system identification all provide strong prior art for defining or recovering state through future behavior. The candidate contribution is the developmental-biology workflow that combines calibrated residual-history testing, a finite-resolution ambiguity representation, and prospective perturbation design.

## Did you prove plants are Markovian?

No. The strongest Arabidopsis result is a narrow predictive screening pattern in one late-L1 FM1 growth task. It is finite-sample, task-, stage-, compartment-, representation-, loss-, and decoder-relative. It does not prove exact conditional independence, universal Markovity, or disappearance of biological memory.

## What did the Arabidopsis reanalysis actually show?

In the released FM1 atlas, current geometry plus the released 25-channel integrated atlas state predicts subsequent lineage growth strongly in the late-L1 task. Adding the released older ancestor state does not provide a stable material incremental gain across the tested lineage-grouped analyses. An earlier L1 window is more estimator- and split-sensitive, so the result should not be generalized into a universal developmental rule.

## Were 25 genes measured longitudinally in the same living cells?

No. The released FM1 gene channels are integrated binary atlas annotations assembled onto the reference from published and newly acquired expression information. They are not 25 simultaneous repeated molecular measurements in every exact tracked living cell.

## What is the simulator result?

A separate prospectively frozen source-simulator experiment in a restricted four-channel model used designed phenotype contexts to recover a hidden signed state under a frozen decoder and preregistered thresholds. This is computational validation of the experimental logic. It is not living-plant validation and does not imply universal two-perturbation sufficiency.

## What is the main mathematical result?

Several precise statements survived audit. Under a monotone experiment metric, adding experiments refines the target-compatible connected ambiguity. Bounded signature error produces a tolerance sandwich and stable merge thresholds in finite graphs. At fixed positive hidden-world resolution, standard compactness/continuity assumptions imply a finite uniformly separating experiment panel. However, connected-ambiguity reduction is not generally submodular: an explicit construction makes greedy experiment selection arbitrarily bad, with ratio `3/n -> 0`. Under additional structure such as a deterministic rooted-tree coverage model, classical greedy guarantees return.

These surrounding mathematical ingredients have strong precedents and are not claimed as newly invented fields.

## What happened to the earlier 224-world result?

The original 224-world generator/source/output bundle was not recovered in the authenticated workspace during the provenance audit. The named historical topology checkpoint and original perturbation-ranking outputs were also not recovered. Those results remain historical project reports and are not presented as currently reproducible evidence.

## Is the later 128-world bundle the same experiment?

No. The later 128 world/context two-context source-validation bundle is a distinct experiment with a recoverable frozen artifact chain. It must not be used as a replacement for the missing historical 224-world study.

## What happened to the `0.272 -> 0.643` result?

The exact historical pair is provenance-incomplete. A direct-source modern reconstruction can reproduce nonlinear late-L1 performance near 0.64, but it cannot regenerate the exact original paired baseline and molecular result from the executable artifact chain associated with the original prose. The exact tuple is therefore not independently reproduced and should not be quoted as such.

## Has this been validated in living plants?

No. Living-plant perturbational validation remains prospective. The current biology lane has a source-audited minimal design, but it is still gated by material availability, cross feasibility, reporter compatibility, inducible-assay transfer, imaging logistics, physiological burden controls, and definition of a legitimate equal-cost/random comparator.

## What would falsify the framework?

Examples include: reproducibly large residual-history value after rich present-state measurement across relevant biological systems; designed perturbations failing to outperform valid baseline-only and equal-cost/random comparators; connected-fiber structure proving unstable to reasonable metrics/discretization; or current numerical results failing independent code-level reproduction.

## Are you claiming to be first?

No. The public novelty statement explicitly avoids “first,” “unprecedented,” and similar priority language. Each component has close precedents. The candidate contribution is the integration and prospective test in a developmental setting.

## What is the strongest evidence today?

The strongest current package consists of: (1) reproducible observed-data reanalysis with a narrow late-L1 FM1 screening pattern and an external incompleteness control; (2) a frozen prospective source-simulator validation; (3) theorem/counterexample work that defines when ambiguity refinement is safe and when greedy design can fail; and (4) a transparent provenance correction that downgrades historical results whose original artifact chains are missing.
