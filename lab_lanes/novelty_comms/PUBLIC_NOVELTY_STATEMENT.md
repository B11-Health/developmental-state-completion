# Public Novelty Statement — N1 Red-Team Version

## Preferred public wording

We are developing and testing a developmental-biology workflow that asks whether a physically measurable present state is sufficient for specified future interventions, using calibrated residual-history prediction as the diagnostic. When it is not sufficient, we represent the remaining joint state–law alternatives as a finite-resolution connected inverse-fiber component and choose perturbations to split that specific residual ambiguity.

The underlying ingredients have strong precedents in predictive-state representations, computational mechanics, observability and structural identifiability, Reeb-space topology, active experimental design, fate prediction, perturbation-response mapping, and evo-devo work on cryptic variation. The candidate contribution is therefore **not** a new definition of state or a new topology. It is the task-specific integration of these ideas into a falsifiable developmental experiment, together with prospective tests of whether the designed perturbations outperform baseline-only and equal-cost/random alternatives.

## Short version

The candidate contribution is a falsifiable experimental workflow for measuring when a developmental state is predictively sufficient and for choosing perturbations against the specific mechanism ambiguity that remains.

## Evidence-safe qualifier

Current support is observational and computational: a direct-source Arabidopsis FM1 reanalysis shows a strong task-specific late-L1 screening pattern but an estimator-dependent earlier window; a Weinreb/Klein lineage analysis acts as an incompleteness control; and a frozen source-simulator experiment prospectively recovered hidden signed state under designed contexts. Living-plant perturbational validation remains the decisive next step.
